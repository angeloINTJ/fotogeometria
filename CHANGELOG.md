# Changelog

**English** | [Português](CHANGELOG.pt-BR.md)

All notable changes to FotoGeometria. Each stage of [the plan](docs/PLANO.md) lands as one pull request and one entry under *Unreleased* until the first release.

## Unreleased

### Stage 3: the editor and its controls

- **The editor.** `src/ui/editor-view.ts`: the preview takes the screen, with a grid that comes and goes. Above it: change the photo, the grid, and the original to compare, which holding the preview also shows (PLANO.md §4). Below it, one tool at a time: Level (tilt and rotation, by hand until lines are traced in stage 4 and detected in stage 6), Edges (Panini and vertical compression) and Lens (the presets 0.5x, 1x and 2x, that is 13, 24 and 48 mm, and the focal length from 10 to 60 mm); then Undo and Restore defaults. The tabs, the sliders and the comparison work from the keyboard, and every value reads in the interface's language ("+2,5°", "70%", "24 mm").
- **The start** opens a photo from the gallery or from the camera.
- **The focal length from EXIF.** `src/io/exif.ts` reads FocalLengthIn35mmFormat with exifr's mini build, loaded on the first photo: a photo opens at its own focal length, held to the control's range, and the lens tool says what the photo records, or that it records none.
- **Undo and what is kept.** A slider dragged is one change for undo, and up to 100 are kept. The edges and the grid are kept between visits in localStorage; a storage that refuses, or holds what the controls cannot take, leaves the defaults.
- **The stage 2 fixed correction is gone**, as the maintainer confirmed: a photo opens unlevelled.
- **Measuring the preview.** With `?medir` in the address, the editor moves Panini and the tilt every frame for 3 seconds and reports the frames per second: 60 on SwiftShader, and 24 when each frame is made 40 ms slower, so the count follows what a frame costs. Stage 3 is done at 30 on a mid-range phone, which has not been measured yet.
- **Browser tests** (`e2e/editor.spec.ts`): each of the five sliders, and the presets, changes the preview; undo brings back each earlier picture byte for byte, and restore the first; holding the preview, or the button from the keyboard, shows the original; the grid, kept for the next visit; the edges kept for the next photo, and the levelling not; the EXIF focal length, or the note that there is none; the camera's input; and `?medir`.
- **Build.** JavaScript from 23,973 to 65,313 B: the editor, 13,705 B more in the page's bundle, and exifr's chunk, 27,635 B, loaded with the first photo. CSS from 3,479 to 5,288 B.
- **Tests.** 239 unit cases and 37 browser cases.

### Stage 2: the reprojection on the GPU, and a page that shows it

- **The shader.** `src/gl/reproject.frag` and `src/gl/renderer.ts`: WebGL2, one triangle over the target, the photo as a texture with mipmaps and trilinear filtering (anisotropic up to 8x where the GPU has it), high precision throughout. It draws to the canvas for the preview, and to a framebuffer read back for the tests and, from stage 5, the export; both are drawn the same way up, so the canvas shows byte for byte what the read-back reads. A lost WebGL context is rebuilt and drawn again (PLANO.md §6).
- **No trigonometry on the GPU.** The plane coordinates, the vertical expansion and the inverse Panini depend on the output column or on the output row alone, so `src/core` computes them in double precision as two tables (`outputColumn`, `outputRow`, and `paniniColumn`, the column half of `paniniInverse`), and the shader only rotates and divides. Evaluated in the shader, the trigonometry put the sampled source 0.139 px from the reference's on SwiftShader, whose `sin` and `cos` the Vulkan rules allow an error of 2⁻¹¹; from the tables, 0.00025 px at worst. `paniniInverse` gives the same bits as before (sha256 of 133,352 outputs, before and after).
- **The page.** `src/ui/`: choose a photo, and see it whole, at the screen's resolution, with the reference case's correction: a pitch of 17.5°, a roll of 0.8°, and the reference's focal length, 480 px on a 1600 px diagonal (12.98 mm, `docs/DECISIONS.md`). The photo opens upright, by its EXIF orientation, and is reduced once when it is past the GPU's texture size or 4096 x 4096 pixels, the canvas area WebKit allowed on iOS until 2024 (`src/io/load-image.ts`). In Brazilian Portuguese, English or Spanish, after the device; every state drawn, opening, a file that is not an image and a browser without WebGL2 among them; in the Ângulo standard, in both themes.
- **The synthetic photo.** `scripts/generate_fixtures.py` also writes a facade grid seen by the reference case's camera (red verticals, blue horizontals, a constant green) and the reference's own correction of it, and `--check` compares both images exactly.
- **Browser tests** (Playwright, `e2e/`, new CI job `e2e`), on SwiftShader: the shader's source positions against the reference's at its samples, in the four parity cases, and against `src/core` on every pixel, 2,308 pixels behind the camera included; the grid's lines where OpenCV draws them, 0.028 and 0.038 px away on average, where half a pixel off measures 0.50; the verticals within 0.015° (the plan asks for 0.2°); the canvas equal to the read-back; the margin, where the shader counts the reference's 1,054,636 valid pixels; the photo drawn unchanged for the before of before/after; the mipmaps, without which the grid drawn at an eighth strays twice as far from the reference averaged over each pixel (11.1 levels against 5.1); and the app opening a photo, upright by its EXIF orientation, reduced from 20 megapixels to 4579 x 3663, refusing a file that is not one, telling a browser without WebGL2, coming back after a lost context, and speaking three languages. Seventeen defects put into the shader, the tables, the read-back, the filtering, the loader and the context's restore each fail between 1 and 15 of the 23; the eighteenth, dropping `imageOrientation: 'from-image'`, passes on Chromium, which applies a JPEG's orientation anyway.
- **CI.** A new job, `e2e`. It and the `fixtures` job time out at 5 minutes, the floor: their first runs took 43 s and 15 s.
- **Tests.** 216 unit cases and 23 browser cases.

### Stage 1: the mathematics of the correction (`src/core/`), held to the reference

- **The modules.** `linalg` (vectors, 3x3 matrices, Rodrigues as OpenCV computes it, and the smallest singular vector by one-sided Jacobi), `camera` (the focal length from the 35 mm equivalent, the lens presets, rays), `panini` (the projection both ways and the vertical compression), `leveling` (the rotation from the vanishing point, the strength, the fine adjustment), `framing` (the output framing, the source pixel of each output pixel, the validity mask) and `correction` (the parameters of PLANO.md §5 and the one function that turns them into a projection).
- **Parity with the reference.** `scripts/generate_fixtures.py` runs the reference's own functions on four cases (the plan's reference case among them) and writes `tests/fixtures/parity.json`: the rotation, the framing, the source coordinates of a 21 x 21 grid of output pixels, the count of valid pixels and the crop rectangles. The TypeScript matches the rotation to 10⁻¹², the framing to 10⁻¹², every sampled coordinate to 10⁻⁶ px (the plan asks for 10⁻² px) and the count of valid pixels exactly, 1,054,636 of them in the reference case. CI regenerates the fixtures under Python 3.12 with the pinned NumPy and OpenCV and compares them (new job `fixtures`).
- **Verticals stay vertical.** Lines of a synthetic facade, seen by a camera with a known pitch and roll and corrected, lean less than 0.2° from the vertical (PLANO.md §7); without the levelling they lean more than 5°, which shows the check bites.
- **The architecture rule, both halves.** ESLint refuses an import from `gl/`, `ui/`, `io/` or `detect/` into `src/core/`, and `tsconfig.core.json` compiles `src/core/` without the DOM's types; `tests/architecture.test.ts` puts each violation back and expects exactly one finding.
- **Mutations.** Five defects put into the core by hand (Panini's latitude, the sign of the down vector, the optical centre, the Rodrigues scale, the pixel centre) each fail between 1 and 16 tests.
- **CI timeouts** are 5 minutes, twice the longest job of the first runs (14 s) rounded up, except the new `fixtures` job, provisional at 10.
- **Tests.** 183 cases.

### Stage 0: the project's structure, its gates and CI

- **The project.** Vite 8, TypeScript 6 in strict mode, ESLint 10 with the type-aware rules of typescript-eslint, Prettier 3 and Vitest 5, every version pinned exactly. `npm run dev` serves a placeholder page in the Ângulo standard; the editor arrives in stages 1 to 5.
- **The reference.** The validated Python, `reference/corrigir_geometria.py`, byte for byte (sha256 `754e5154…`, pinned by a test). Given a vanishing point built from a pitch of 17.5° and a roll of 0.8°, it reproduces the plan's reference output, 711×1016, without the reference photo.
- **The gates, each with its mutations in `tests/tools/`.** A secret scan (key files, private key material, access tokens, literal credentials); the Ângulo standard where a machine can check it (the pinned copies of simut-rx, and the app's CSS: no literal colour, gradient, card shadow, stray radius, distance off the 4 px grid or type off the scale; no emoji; flat-square badges); the third-party notices, generated from `tools/third-party.json`; and an exact size budget of the build, which fails when a category grows or shrinks without being recorded.
- **The interface standard.** `src/styles/angulo.css`, the display font and `docs/ANGULO.md` are pinned copies of simut-rx. The theme follows the device from the first paint.
- **The repository.** MIT licence; README, CONTRIBUTING and Code of Conduct in English, Portuguese and Spanish; SECURITY; issue and pull request templates; Dependabot for npm and the GitHub Actions; a path labeler for pull requests; pre-commit hooks; and CI with one job per gate.
- **Tests.** 42 cases.
